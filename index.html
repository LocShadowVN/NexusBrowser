Features

Everything a browser needs. Nothing it doesn't.

No bundled search deals, no upsells, no "recommended by Nexus" cards. Just the parts a browser is actually for.

Encrypted vault

Passwords are sealed with AES-256-GCM and an Argon2id-derived key. The master password lives in RAM, zeroized on lock — and there's no cloud to breach.

Private tabs, actually private

Private tabs skip history, session restore and password saving by design — not by toggling three settings and hoping.

Secure DNS

Domain lookups go through encrypted DNS-over-HTTPS — Cloudflare, Google, or your own resolver — with a TTL cache so pages don't pay the round-trip twice.

16-thread downloads

File links are detected automatically and pulled down in 16 parallel range requests with a live progress bar. Single-stream fallback when the server can't.

Extension support

Nexus implements enough of chrome.runtime that many Manifest-style content scripts run unmodified from nexus_extensions/.

Built to idle light

Background tabs are suspended after five minutes — the connection pool is released, the page stays cached and snaps back instantly when you click it.

Shield

Blocking, at two layers

Most browsers block inside the page and hope that's enough. Nexus also stops known ad and tracking domains at the network layer — the request never leaves your machine.

Layer 1 · network

Sinkhole

Known ad/tracker domains (doubleclick, taboola, criteo, google-analytics…) are dropped before DNS is even queried. The request is refused locally — nothing to log on the other end.

Layer 2 · the page

In-page hooks

What slips through the network layer gets stopped in the page: fetch, XHR, beacons, WebSockets — and ad script/image/iframe nodes are stripped as they appear.

FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Is Nexus a drop-in replacement for Brave or Firefox?

For everyday browsing — reading, search, video, most web apps — yes. Nexus renders pages through your OS's native WebView instead of shipping its own engine, which is where the memory savings come from. A small number of very complex web apps may behave slightly differently.

What exactly does the Shield button show me?

Tap it and you see the count, plus every domain blocked on the current page — grouped by type (Ad / Tracker) and hit count — and the blocked-list totals from the network layer. You can switch the tab between Strict, Balanced and Off right there.

Does Secure DNS slow browsing down?

Resolved names are cached with their real TTL, so each domain costs one encrypted round-trip and then nothing. If the resolver is unreachable, Nexus falls back to system DNS rather than failing to load pages.

If I forget my vault master password, can you recover it?

No — and that's the point. Passwords are encrypted with a key derived from your master password on your machine. There's no reset link, no support ticket, no backdoor.

Who's behind this?

No company — an individual maintainer plus whoever from the community wants to contribute. There's no roadmap dictated by investors, and no plan to monetize it later.
